# Memory Horizon - Outcome Definition

## User outcome

A person or researcher can see, in time rather than vague impression, how reliably newly experienced events are being retained: what was remembered after 30 seconds, 2 minutes, 5 minutes, 10 minutes, and so on.

## Ultimate vision

A lightweight, browser-native episodic-memory measurement engine hidden inside a simple game. It can support repeated research measurements without requiring a laboratory interface, while preserving raw observations so scientists and clinicians can judge the evidence rather than trusting an opaque score.

Potential future research domains include concussion/TBI recovery, aging, anesthesia recovery, stroke rehabilitation, medication effects and general cognitive science. These are hypotheses for validation, not approved indications.

## Primary outputs

- Event-by-event retention timeline.
- Delay-specific free recall, cued recall and recognition.
- Response latency.
- Estimated retention horizon/curve with uncertainty.
- Session-to-session change using alternate forms.
- Observer annotations kept distinct from player responses.

## Success metrics

POC:
- >=12 scorable unique events in a deterministic demonstration.
- >=95% synthetic timing/event linkage integrity.
- Zero scored event reuse after retirement.
- Export fully reconstructs session state.

Prototype:
- repeat-test practice effect measured rather than assumed away.
- test-retest reliability and completion burden quantified.
- mobile completion and abandonment measured.

## Anti-outcomes

- Not a diagnosis of concussion, dementia or any disease.
- Not proof of neurological recovery or fitness to return to sport.
- Not a replacement for emergency assessment or validated clinical instruments.
- Not an engagement game optimized at the expense of measurement validity.
- Not collection of identifiable health data by default.

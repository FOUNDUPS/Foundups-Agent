# Japan Hyperscaler Report (JHR)

JHR is the Project eSingularity research and publication lane for Japan's hyperscale data-center buildout.

## Mission

Track the evidence that determines where and how Japan's AI-compute infrastructure is being built, and explain why Fukui should make data-center strategy a present-tense economic-development priority rather than react after hyperscale land, power, and policy decisions are already locked in.

Primary beats:

- national METI/GX/Watt-Bit policy and grid constraints;
- hyperscale campuses, operators, capacity, land and power commitments;
- Chiba/Inzai as the leading social and land-use case study;
- farmer, resident, temple, neighborhood, planning and municipal responses;
- prefectural and municipal rules, incentives, approvals and opposition;
- Fukui City / Fukui Prefecture implications and preemption opportunities;
- local radio, newspapers, councils and other civic signals;
- the alternative distributed 1-20 MW COG DC / eSingularity architecture.

## WSP 97 publication boundary

Running the researcher is **not** evidence that a report exists or is publishable.

The pipeline is:

`DISCOVER -> CAPTURE -> CORROBORATE -> SCORE -> DRAFT -> VERIFY -> PUBLISH_CANDIDATE`

A public report requires a retained source ledger, explicit claim/source bindings, a significance decision, and a verification receipt. Weak signal becomes `NO_REPORT`; it must not be padded into content just to satisfy a cadence.

## Cadence

JHR is assessment-driven. `python main.py` may start a bounded, non-blocking assessment. The default publication target is monthly, but a material policy, grid, land-use or community event can justify a special issue. A month with no significant verified change may legitimately produce no issue.

Runtime research is written to an outbox outside the tracked public content tree. Promotion to the public eSingularity report feed is a separate evidence gate.

FOR MEMBER OFFICES AND COMMITTEE STAFF

Legislative oversight roadmap

Use this proposed roadmap to start a House or Senate inquiry, build a reliable record and decide what Congress can do if the Department of Justice does not act. A state referral is a separate decision under state law.

Start here. Ask the office’s legislative director or committee counsel to identify the law, program or funding decision at issue, the committee with jurisdiction and the next decision-maker. Bring the known facts, source links and open questions. Use the office’s approved channels for sensitive material.

Jump to a decision
  1. Scope the inquiry
  2. Assign owners and approve collection
  3. Build and challenge the record
  4. Act within Congress
  5. Decide whether to refer

1. Scope the inquiry

Owner. The member office and counsel for the committee with jurisdiction. Record. A short memo with the legislative purpose, governing rules, key questions, known facts, evidence gaps and proposed lead office.

Identify a decision Congress can make, such as amending an oversight requirement or reviewing how an agency administers a program. Confirm the current chamber rules and the committee’s own rules. If committee jurisdiction or legislative purpose is unclear, resolve it before opening a formal inquiry.

Legislative purpose memo · House rules · Senate rules

2. Set up the inquiry and evidence process

Owner. The authorized committee decision-maker and designated counsel. Record. An operating plan naming the approver, staff lead, record custodian, permitted collection methods and access controls.

Start with public records and properly approved voluntary requests. Before compulsory steps, document the specific authority, approval, scope and service requirements. For a shared hub, agree in writing which office holds each record, who may see it and who may approve transfer. Each chamber retains its own powers and approvals.

Committee operating plan · Shared hub charter · Model evidence request

3. Build and challenge the record

Owner. Assigned staff and custodian, with a second reviewer and counsel. Record. An evidence register, a chronology and a report separating documented facts, interpretations, disputed facts and unanswered questions.

Preserve originals and record sources, custody and restrictions. Check contrary evidence and give affected parties an appropriate chance to respond under the office’s procedures. Hold protected or disputed material for counsel. If a fact cannot be supported, narrow the finding or record the gap.

Evidence report · Evidence review checklist · Privilege and enforcement guide

4. Act on findings within Congress

Owner. The committee or other authorized congressional office. Record. An approved hearing, report, legislative proposal, appropriations follow-up or audit request, with an owner and review date.

Congress can pursue these steps without waiting for federal prosecution. Consider a GAO audit request when the issue concerns federal program performance or spending. If a witness or custodian resists, ask counsel about narrower requests, accommodations and any available enforcement route. The federal criminal contempt statute routes certification to a U.S. attorney.

GAO congressional request process · 2 U.S.C. § 194 · January 6 record and staff readiness

5. Decide whether to refer a state-law matter

Owner. Authorized committee officials and counsel for disclosure. The receiving office controls its own response. Record. A written send, hold or close decision and, if approved, a versioned referral packet and delivery receipt.

Confirm the state statute, facts connecting the conduct to that state, venue, timing and the recipient’s authority. The proper office may be a state attorney general, county prosecutor or regulator. Counsel should screen the packet for privilege, immunity, secrecy, privacy and other disclosure limits. Verify the receiving office’s secure intake channel before transferring only approved records.

Keep the legislative work moving if the referral has no state-law basis, material cannot be disclosed or the recipient declines. A referral carries no obligation to investigate or prosecute. A delivery receipt confirms delivery alone.

State jurisdiction guide · State referral packet · New York Executive Law § 63 · Washington RCW 43.10.232

Weekly staff review

  • Log the current stage, next decision and responsible office, date due, open evidence gaps, and items held for legal review.
  • Track each congressional action and its follow-up separately from any referral. Distinguish authorized, delivered, acknowledged and closed. Record a recipient’s disposition only when officially confirmed.
  • Have authorized staff follow up through the verified channel when appropriate. Record changes and corrections in the approved office system. Publish updates only after disclosure approval.

Example: a missing grant report

Suppose a federal grant program’s public files lack a required report. Committee staff can identify the reporting rule, ask the agency for the report and its explanation, and check whether the omission is recurring. The committee can consider a hearing or changes to reporting requirements based on the reviewed record. A missing report alone supplies no state-law basis. If separate evidence raises a state-law issue, counsel can evaluate that issue on its own facts and authority.

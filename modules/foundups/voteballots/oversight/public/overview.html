FOR CONGRESSIONAL STAFF AND COUNSEL

From a credible lead to a lawful referral

A roadmap for the incoming House to keep oversight moving when the Department of Justice is a bottleneck.

  1. Pain

    Attorney General Todd Blanche previously defended President Donald Trump in three criminal cases. In our view, that raises an impartiality concern in matters involving the president.

  2. Solution

    Congress builds the record. With counsel review and committee approval, it can refer evidence of a separate state-law matter to a state attorney general or other office with jurisdiction.

  3. Outcome

    Congress can act on its findings without waiting for DOJ. State offices decide independently whether to investigate or enforce their laws.

HOW THE MODEL WORKS

One evidence hub. Separate decision makers.

  1. Document the lead

    Record the source, dates, the potential issue and what remains unverified.

  2. Review the evidence

    Assign the matter to a committee with a legislative purpose. Preserve original records, custody and alternative explanations.

  3. Get counsel’s decision

    Check authority, applicable law, jurisdiction, privilege and what may be shared. Record the approval for each released item.

  4. Act on the findings

    Consider hearings, a report, or legislative changes. If a separate state-law basis exists, transfer only authorized material and record the receipt.

STATE REFERRAL

Find the office with authority.

Congress can pursue legislative oversight even when federal prosecutors decline to act. Federal criminal contempt still goes to a U.S. attorney under 2 U.S.C. § 194. A separate state referral depends on the facts and the state's own law.

The proper recipient may be an attorney general, local prosecutor, or regulator. Counsel must match the facts to the statute, jurisdiction and disclosure rules before transfer. The receiving office decides whether to investigate, pursue a civil remedy or prosecute.

INDEPENDENT CIVIC PROPOSAL

How Congress can investigate and inform the public.

When questions about government conduct go unanswered, congressional committees can gather evidence for legislative work and explain what the record supports. This blueprint proposes a shared evidence process that can operate even when federal prosecutors do not act.

THE PROPOSED WORKFLOW

From a question to a reviewable record

  1. Assign the question

    Define the legislative purpose and choose a committee with jurisdiction. House Rules X and XI govern House committee work. Senate Rules XXV and XXVI separately address Senate committee jurisdiction and procedures for hearings, testimony and authorized subpoenas.

  2. Preserve and challenge the evidence

    A proposed shared hub would index sources, dates, custody and counterevidence. Committee teams would keep their own decision authority. Independent review, counsel and procedural safeguards would precede release.

  3. Report or refer within the law

    Publish supported findings and uncertainties where disclosure is permitted. If evidence points to a possible state-law issue, counsel would check jurisdiction and sharing restrictions before referring it. The receiving office decides what action, if any, its law permits.

## 3. COGDC / eSingularity framing

**COGDC = Community-Owned Green Data Center.**

Working metaphor for Japanese explanation:
- Rice fields grow food for people.
- Data centers grow/provide compute for AI.
- A community-scale COGDC should return value, heat, knowledge, and capability to the region rather than operate as an extractive hyperscale facility.

Core value lanes:
- preserve onsen / existing asset
- regional compute
- education and university access
- research
- startup incubation
- local business enablement
- tourism/cultural activation
- potential waste-heat reuse
- potential onsen/thermal-network extensions only after engineering validation

Do not promise community hot-water distribution before engineering confirms temperature, flow, losses, pumping, health regulation, ownership, and economics.

## 4. Economic-development argument

Key decision-theory principle:

> As long as the structure exists, Fukui retains an option. Demolition converts that option into a cost and removes future upside.

The comparison must be:

`DEMOLITION / EMPTY-SITE OUTCOME` **vs** `PRESERVATION / PRODUCTIVE-REUSE OPTION`

Not simply old-building cost vs renovation cost.

Economic impact model still needed:
- direct operating revenue
- city fiscal impact
- jobs / local procurement
- university and research value
- startup formation
- tourism / lodging / food-service revival
- events / culture
- surrounding property/business effects
- thermal-energy opportunities
- long-term regional compute value

Need to show downside/risks of reuse too:
- capex
- asbestos/remediation
- landowner complexity
- technical uncertainty
- investor execution risk
- operating risk
- time to feasibility

Credibility rule: label assumptions and scenarios; never present projections as facts.

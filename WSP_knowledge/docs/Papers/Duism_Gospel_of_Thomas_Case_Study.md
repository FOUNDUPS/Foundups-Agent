# Duism Applied to Christianity — Gospel of Thomas Case Study

**Status:** Living paper / 012 interpretation, 2026-10-28. Add further sayings here rather than enlarging the metaphysical foundation.

## Sources and limitations
The *Gospel of Thomas* is a sayings collection transmitted in Coptic with Greek fragments. Its composition, dating, and relationship to other Christian traditions are disputed. It is not an unmediated recording of Jesus. Translation wording varies. [Lambdin translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/gthlamb.html) is a reading reference, not exclusive authority.

## Saying 3: the kingdom within and outside
Jesus challenges locating the kingdom exclusively in sky or sea, and speaks of it as within and outside. **012's Duist interpretation:** searching for a supernatural external controller misses the already-present system of which people are expressions. **Boundary:** the saying does not name Duism, entanglement, or modern cosmology.

## Saying 13: “Who am I?” as a comprehension test
In the saying Jesus asks disciples to compare him with someone or something. Simon Peter says a righteous angel; Matthew a wise philosopher; Thomas says his mouth cannot describe whom Jesus resembles. Jesus takes Thomas aside and says three things, which Thomas declines to repeat to the others, warning of stones and fire.

### 012's staged game-theoretic reading
The monk imagines the teacher anticipating death or departure and assessing who can preserve his teaching. This is a **storytelling premise**, not a documented historical event. Peter's answer is read as religious coloring; Matthew's as philosophical coloring; Thomas's as recognition that the categories cannot capture the referent. The private disclosure creates **information asymmetry**; the teacher's problem becomes selecting between comprehension and organizational capacity. Thomas's inability or refusal to transmit the private message raises the succession paradox.

**Historical controls:** Saying 13 does not place the exchange at the Last Supper; no evidence here identifies Peter and Matthew as two formal factions; Paul was not one of the Twelve; Simon Peter is one person, not two. Do not say Thomas was silent.

## sahdu
**sahdu** is the monk's lowercase descriptive coinage, from **sadhu → sahdu** by swapping d and h, mnemonic **“say Du”**. It describes someone speaking the truth of the system, not a title or supernatural status. Capitalize only at sentence start. Do not substitute *Sadducee*, *sadhu*, or *Sadu*.

# Duism Applied to Religion — Comparative Framework

**Status:** Working research and teaching paper, 2026-10-28. **Not a historical or scientific consensus statement.**

## Purpose and method
Duism treats **Du** as the underlying system/reality. Religious traditions can be studied as different **colorings**: human narratives, images, personifications, moral systems, institutions, and rituals that interpret reality. The subtractive method asks what observation or existential question remains after separating supernatural assertions, inherited authority, and culturally specific imagery. This does **not** establish that all religions secretly teach Duism or that their adherents must discard their traditions.

See [the metaphysical foundation](Duism_Metaphysics_Foundation.md) and [the two-state Dukkyō lineage](Dukkyou_Duism_Subtractive_Lineage.md): **独境** is the seeking/colored duist state; **毒教** is the uncolored Duist state and canonical Japanese name for Duism. Both are deliberate, not drift.

## Comparative research map

### Animism
Study relationships with animals, landscapes, ancestors, and other-than-human agents. Ask how agency is distributed through the lived environment, without equating indigenous traditions with physics or treating them as primitive.

### Polytheisms
Study plural divine personifications, local cults, cosmologies, and ritual practices. Ask how multiple named agents express distinct functions or experiences. Avoid assuming a uniform system across cultures.

### Monotheisms
Study the singular divine referent, transcendence/immanence, revelation, and institutional authority. The Duist comparison asks how the referent becomes anthropomorphized or “colored,” but must distinguish what practitioners actually claim from the monk's interpretation.

**Important historical qualification:** animism → polytheism → monotheism is an organizing route for this paper, **not** an established universal evolutionary ladder. These forms overlap, coexist, and resist simple classification.

## Tradition-specific research sections
- **Christianity:** [Gospel of Thomas case study](Duism_Gospel_of_Thomas_Case_Study.md), especially Sayings 3 and 13; compare with canonical gospel traditions without presuming Thomas is a verbatim historical transcript.
- **Judaism:** future study of divine naming, covenant, transcendence, and interpretation.
- **Islam:** future study of tawhid, divine attributes, and the relationship between revelation and metaphysical language.
- **Hindu traditions:** future study of Brahman, atman, nondual schools, and plurality; avoid flattening distinct traditions.
- **Buddhist traditions:** future study of dependent origination, emptiness, and non-self; do not casually equate emptiness with a quantum field.
- **Daoist traditions:** future study of naming, Dao, and non-anthropomorphic language; **Duism is not Daoism**.
- **Indigenous and other traditions:** study with primary sources and living practitioner perspectives, without treating them as stages superseded by later religions.

## Analytical rubric for every case
1. **Primary text / historical source**: exact passage, translation, provenance, dating uncertainty.
2. **Insider understanding**: what practitioners and scholars say the tradition means.
3. **012's Duist reading**: what “coloring” is proposed, and what remains after subtraction.
4. **Game-theoretic hypothesis**: incentives, coordination, leadership, transmission, institutional survival.
5. **Evidence boundary**: distinguish textual fact, historical inference, metaphor, and untested physics.
6. **Ethics**: freedom to color or not color, no coercion, no harm.

This is a research scaffold, not a declaration that the historical analysis is finished.
